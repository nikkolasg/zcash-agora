How it works

In plain words. The technical page says the same with the names of the pieces.

The promise

An isoloir is the French word for a voting booth: the curtain you step behind so nobody sees your ballot. Zcash Agora is a voting booth for the internet, with four promises:

  1. Only real people vote, and each one once. You prove, with your passport, that you are one person. You cannot register the same passport twice.
  2. Nobody sees your ballot before the close. Not even us. Your choice is locked with a key that does not exist yet: the signature a public randomness network will publish at the closing time, and not before.
  3. Nobody can tell which ballot is yours. Ever. After the close, ballots open, but each one is signed by an anonymous pseudonym that cannot be linked to your passport, your registration, or your pseudonym in another poll.
  4. Anyone can recount. Everything, including refused messages, is in a public log, signed and timestamped in Bitcoin. The result is a calculation on public data. You do not have to trust our number: redo it.

What you need

  • A passport with a chip (the small symbol on the cover). Most passports issued since the 2010s have one.
  • A phone that can read it (NFC: any recent iPhone or Android) with the free ZKPassport app.
  • Any browser, on the phone or a computer, to vote.

No wallet, no ZEC, no account. You will not be asked your name.

Step by step

  1. Open the vote page. It shows a QR code. Scan it with the ZKPassport app.
  2. Scan your passport. The app reads the chip over NFC and, on your phone, produces a proof that the passport is valid and unexpired, plus a number unique to the passport. Your name, photo, date of birth and nationality never leave the phone; the proof contains none of them.
  3. Keep your key. Your browser makes a secret key, the one thing that will later link you to your ballot, and only on your device. You can save it as a passkey (synced by your phone or password manager) or write down 12 words. Lose it and you can still not vote twice, but you cannot vote from another device.
  4. Register. The proof and your key's fingerprint go, through public relays, to the ledger. The ledger checks the proof, refuses the passport if it already registered, and adds you to the list of voters. The list is public, but it holds only unique numbers, never names.
  5. Vote. Pick an option. Your browser proves, in a way that reveals nothing about which registration is yours, that you are on the list, and seals your choice to the closing time. It takes a few seconds on a phone, a bit longer on an old one.
  6. Get a receipt. The ledger answers with a signed acknowledgement naming the place your ballot will have in the log. Your browser later checks the log and raises a public complaint if the ballot is missing.
  7. The close. At the closing time the randomness network publishes its round. Every ballot opens. The count is published, with the exact list of what was counted and what was not, and anyone can redo it.

If you do not want a relay to see your network address, use Tor or a VPN. Your ballot's secrecy does not depend on it.

Things to know

  • One ballot, no changes. In this poll you cannot change your vote after casting it. (A correction would open at the close like any ballot and reveal a pattern, so the design forbids it.)
  • Your ballot becomes public at the close, anonymously. That is deliberate: it is what lets anyone recount without a key holder. What becomes public is a choice next to a pseudonym, not a person.
  • Keep your 12 words private. Whoever has them can prove which ballot is yours (after the close) and vote in future polls as you.
  • It binds no one. This is a measurement. The ecosystem decides what to make of it.
