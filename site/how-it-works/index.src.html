Your vote stays secret.
Even from us.

Four simple ideas, and anyone can check they hold.

Your passport stays on your phone.

The ledger receives only valid passport and a unique number

{{anim:passport}}

The ZKPassport app reads the chip and proves, on the phone, that the passport is real and unexpired. Your name, photo, birth date and nationality never leave it. The number lets the same passport register once, and says nothing else. One person, one vote.

Your ballot travels incognito.

{{anim:ballot-path}}

Your browser proves you are on the list without saying who, signs the ballot with a pseudonym derived from a secret only your device holds, and posts it to public Nostr relays run by others. Your address stays there: the ledger reads the sealed ballot among the rest.

Sealed until the close, by an independent clock.

{{anim:count}}

Each ballot is locked to a future round of the drand randomness beacon, run by a committee that has nothing to do with Zcash (Cloudflare, EPFL, Kudelski, Protocol Labs and others) and does not know its beacon serves this poll. The key does not exist before the closing time, so nobody can read a ballot early, including us. At the close anyone opens the ballots, each under its pseudonym, and counts.

Anyone can recount everything.

{{anim:pipe}}

Every registration, every ballot, every refusal and the result go into a public log, signed and dated in Bitcoin. Your browser can redo every check, from the first registration to the result.

Audit the vote

What this means for you

Nothing about your passport is learned.A proof that it is valid, and a number unique to it, salted so that even the state that issued it cannot recognize it. Not your name, not your nationality, not your face.
Nobody in Zcash can open your vote.Not us, not any Zcash organization: the ballots open at the closing time, when the independent drand network publishes its round, and not before.
Your vote carries a pseudonym, not you.The pseudonym comes from a secret on your device. Nothing links it to your passport, or to your pseudonym in another poll.
The server does not even see your address.Ballots go through public relays. The ledger reads them there, without knowing where they came from.

What it does not do

Ballots are readable at the close.Each one opens under its pseudonym, which is what lets anyone recount without a key holder. It reveals a choice, never a person.
Your 12 words can prove your vote.After the close, whoever holds them can show how that pseudonym voted. A vote buyer could demand them. Keep them to yourself.
It takes a chip passport and an NFC phone.Passports are the first proof of personhood we accept; the registry format takes others as they come.
Registering takes a face match.The salted number that keeps your registration unknown even to the state that issued your passport requires it.

And it binds no one: a poll here is a measurement offered to the people who decide.
