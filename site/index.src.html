What do the people of Zcash think?

Zcash polls today count coins: a ballot weighs what its caster holds. Zcash Agora counts people: one valid passport, one anonymous ballot, sealed until the close by a clock nobody here controls, and recounted by anyone. The two counts are shown side by side. Neither decides. Both inform.

Why a second count

"98.6% against" is a true sentence about the voting weight. It says nothing about how many humans that weight belongs to, and nobody knows, because a coinholder poll cannot know. Zcash was not built as a shareholder meeting: its roots are cypherpunk, and the protections it gives are for persons, equally, whatever they hold. A decision that looks at one count only is blind in one eye. The full argument.

This is not a replacement for coinholder polling and it does not pretend to bind anyone. It is the other half of the picture, measured with at least the same rigour: every registration, every ballot, every refusal and the result are in a public log that anyone can recount.

How a vote goes

  1. Prove you are one person, once. Scan your passport's chip with the ZKPassport app on your phone. The app proves on the phone that the passport is valid and derives a number unique to it. No name, no nationality, no photo leaves the phone.
  2. Vote behind the curtain. Your browser proves, without revealing which registration is yours, that you are in the registry, and seals your choice to a future time with the drand randomness beacon. Nobody, including us, can open it before the poll closes.
  3. Anyone recounts. At the close, the beacon's round is published, every ballot opens under an anonymous pseudonym, and the count is a function of public data. Download the log, run the audit, get the same number.

In plain words · In technical words · Compared with the coinholder stack

Passports are one way to count people, not the only one. They exclude people without a biometric passport or an NFC phone, and the issuing state could learn that one of its passports registered (never how it voted). We think a count of capital alone is worse than a count of passport holders, and we are open to other proofs of personhood. Who can vote, and why.
