Technically

Zcash Agora is a deployment of isoloir, an open protocol for anonymous polls with passport eligibility. This page summarizes the protocol and names the parameters of this ledger. The normative text is isoloir's SPEC.md; section numbers below (§) refer to it. The threat map is DESIGN.md.

In one paragraph

A ledger is one append-only log (an RFC 6962 Merkle tree with Ed25519-signed checkpoints, C2SP signed-note format), mirrored as static files in a public git repository read by commit, with every checkpoint timestamped in Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps (§4, §15). A registry is a passport rule and a Merkle tree of the registrations that satisfied it (§5). A voter registers by proving with ZKPassport that their passport satisfies the rule, obtaining a document-unique identifier uid; the ingest verifies the proof with ZKPassport's open verifier and refuses a uid already seen (§6). A ballot carries a Noir/UltraHonk zero-knowledge proof that a leaf of the registry tree belongs to the prover, a per-poll pseudonym derived from the voter's secret, and the choice committed and time-locked to a drand round (§9, §7.2). Messages travel through public Nostr relays or the ingest's HTTPS endpoint; every one is acknowledged with a signed receipt naming the checkpoint that will hold it (§11). At the close, anyone fetches the drand beacon, opens every ballot, and posts the tally; the ingest verifies every opening before logging it (§10.4). An auditor recomputes everything from the log, the relays and the mirror (§16).

Eligibility

  • Provider: ZKPassport. The app reads the passport chip over NFC, verifies the issuing state's signature chain against ZKPassport's certificate registry, and produces an UltraHonk proof on the phone. The rule here: document_type: passport, require_expiry: true (the document is unexpired on the day of the proof), no minimum age, no nationality constraint.
  • Unique identifier: the non-salted identifier, derived (Poseidon2) from the document data, the registry's domain (zcash-agora.org) and scope (agora/registry/1). The same passport gives the same uid for this registry, so it registers once, ever. A salted identifier would let one passport register twice.
  • Binding: the proof's custom_data is H(T_reg, registry, R) for a throwaway secp256k1 key R drawn before the scan; R signs the registration body (BIP340). A relay that saw the proof cannot replay it under a leaf of its own (§6).
  • Leaf: ID = H(T_id, registry, s) for the voter secret s (from a passkey's PRF output or 12 BIP39 words), in a Poseidon2 Merkle tree of depth 20 (§5, §14).
  • Verification: the ingest runs ZKPassport's verifier (a Node sidecar on the open SDK) on every registration; so does the authority before opening anything, and so does the audit.

The ballot

The circuit for this poll is choice4_tlock_linked_d20 (Noir, UltraHonk with the noir-recursive verifier target; nargo 1.0.0-beta.22, bb 5.0.0). Its public inputs are the registry root, the poll's identifier and mask, the pseudonym nullifier = H(T_null, poll, s), the value commitment vcmt = H(T_cmt, salt, H(v_0..v_3)), and bhash, the SHA-256 of the time-locked blob reduced modulo the field. It proves that the leaf is in the tree at a root no older than anchor_age (24 h), that the pseudonym is derived from the same secret, and that the committed values encode exactly one valid choice (or the blank) for the poll's mask (§9.1).

The blob is a 16-byte key encrypted with tlock (identity-based encryption on BLS12-381 to the poll's drand round) followed by {"salt","values"} sealed under that key with ChaCha20-Poly1305 (§7.2). The proof binds the blob's hash: a relay cannot swap it, and only the voter can make their own ballot void. Doing the BLS12-381 arithmetic inside a BN254 circuit would cost millions of constraints, so the circuit commits to the values and the blob is checked at the opening instead: a ballot whose blob does not open to its commitment is listed as void and not counted (§10.4).

UltraHonk uses a universal structured reference string (Aztec's); there is no per-circuit ceremony and nothing of ours to trust. Anyone can rebuild the circuit from circuits/gen.py and compare the verification key hash the poll names.

What the League could do: a threshold of its nodes could compute the round's signature early. What they would learn: exactly what everyone learns at the round, earlier. They cannot alter a ballot, forge one, or change the count. What they cannot do: anything with a key that does not exist yet, which is the normal case. drand has produced a round every three seconds since 2023 and the round, once published, is public for ever, so a tally can always be recomputed.

The log and the mirror

  • Entries: ledger, registry, register, poll, ballot, reject, tally, close. Every refusal is logged with the refused body and the reason, so the auditor judges every message the ingest judged (§11.4).
  • Checkpoints at most every 60 s when the log grew, at least every 10 min regardless; entries within a checkpoint are ordered by body hash, so the log does not reveal arrival order (§4).
  • The mirror is static files in a public git repository read through jsDelivr by commit; the checkpoint names the commit. Setups and tallies carry inclusion proofs: the mirror cannot serve a key, a channel or a result the log does not hold (§15).
  • Every checkpoint note is stamped with OpenTimestamps and upgraded until it carries a Bitcoin attestation; the audit checks the stamps against block headers (§4, §16).
  • Receipts: a message is stored, then acknowledged with a signed ack naming its checkpoint, then logged in the same transaction as the checkpoint. A missing body is a complaint anyone can judge (§11.2, §11.3). The relays keep copies, so withholding is provable (§16).

Transport

Every message is a signed Nostr event (NIP-01, kinds 7100 to 7106) whatever carries it. This ledger lists three public relays and a direct HTTPS endpoint. A voter ranks the relays by a hash of s; registrations go to the first, ballots to the next, so no single relay sees both (§11.1). With relays the browser never contacts the operator; with the direct endpoint the operator sees the voter's address and timing, and no third party keeps a copy.

Results

The poll is closed: no tally before the close. At the first checkpoint at or after closes the ingest logs a close entry. The authority (ours, or anyone's) fetches the beacon, verifies it under the chain's public key, opens every counted ballot, and posts {values, counted, void, beacon}; the ingest re-checks every opening before logging it. Two correct tallies are identical, and a browser checks the beacon itself while the openings are an auditor's job (§10.4).

Limits, honestly

  • The operator is one process. It can refuse service and it decides what each checkpoint holds. It cannot read, alter or forge a ballot, register anyone, or drop an acknowledged message quietly. It could fork the log for some readers; the audit collects notes from relays and mirror and fails on two notes of one index with different roots (DESIGN 3.1). Anchoring the log in a chain (Catena) is designed, not deployed.
  • Ballots are public at the close, pseudonymously. A voter who gives up s proves their ballot after the close; the same holds for a coinholder voter who gives up a hotkey. The time-lock delays such a proof; it does not prevent it.
  • One ballot per voter, no corrections. Under time-lock a correction would open in clear and is a pattern of its own (§8).
  • Passports only. ZKPassport also verifies ID cards, but isoloir supports passports only for now (DESIGN §2).
  • Scale. The ingest holds the log in memory (about 100k ballots); the browser rebuilds the registry tree (about 100k voters); a tree of depth 20 holds a million leaves.
  • Not post-quantum. The time-lock is pairing-based. Since every ballot becomes public at the round anyway, a future quantum adversary learns nothing more than the public does; the registration proofs and the ballot proofs hide the passport ↔ leaf and leaf ↔ ballot links, and their security assumptions are those of UltraHonk on BN254.
