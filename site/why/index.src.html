Why this exists

A coinholder poll answers one question: what do the coins want? Nobody asks the other one: what do the people want? Zcash Agora asks it, and puts the two answers side by side.

What the coinholder poll measured

In February 2026 the coinholder poll voted 98.6% against Zcash Shielded Assets. The same week, the Zcash Foundation's advisory panel voted 70.4% for them. Here is what sits behind the two numbers.

The weight was concentrated. The largest ballot alone was 7.4% of the vote. Twelve ballots made up half of it. The whole losing side weighed less than the largest ballot. None of this is a flaw. The poll was built to weigh coins, and it did. It is just not a count of people.

So we have two polls, two opposite answers, and no count of the people of Zcash. One counts ZEC. The other counts a panel of about 200 picked by one foundation. The forum had 842 active members last month.

How the number is used

The 98.6% has become the governance number of Zcash. It opens the case to end Zcash Community Grants and send its share of the block reward to the Network Sustainability Mechanism. Since October, coinholder votes also move money directly, through retroactive grants.

The fight over ZCG is now going to a vote. On 5 November the five organizations that run the coinholder voting chain set a timeline: proposals by 8 November, polls of coinholders and community panels from 16 to 30 November, and a network upgrade if there is rough consensus. On the table are ending ZCG's funding stream and keeping a lockbox, ending ZCG outright, keeping its share for adoption grants, and ending the whole dev fund. ZCG withdrew its own plan on 3 November. Once again the two counts will be coins and a panel.

None of this is wrong. Coinholders carry the risk of the currency, and their view deserves a good measure. But when the coin count is the only count, whoever holds or buys the most ZEC decides. The author of the latest fix to the coinholder poll puts it plainly: it "was meant to be one voice among a few", and "lately it's being treated as the voice that settles things". Nobody ever asks how many people stand behind a position.

Why count people, and why in Zcash

Zcash is cypherpunk. It exists so that a person can be let alone, whether they hold a little or a lot. Weighing voices by holdings is what shareholder meetings do. A count of people is closer to where Zcash comes from.

A passport cannot be bought in bulk. One person is one vote, whatever they hold, and the vote costs nothing. The coinholder poll required moving ZEC into the Orchard pool during a window, so people with coins on an exchange, or with no coins, were not counted there either.

Zcash already wants a people count. ZCAP is one, with a few hundred members picked by one organization. zec.rocks has announced Coinperson Voting, one vote per coinholder, for December, and its thread is a long argument about how to stop one person from voting a thousand times. Zcash Agora is the open version of the idea: anyone with a passport, no list to be put on, run by nobody in the dispute, and the passport is what stops the thousand votes.

People should not have to vote in public. A forum poll is public and easy to stuff. Here your ballot is sealed until the close and opens under a pseudonym. A privacy coin's people deserve a voting booth.

How it is done

You prove you hold a valid passport with the ZKPassport app. Your name never leaves your phone, only a number unique to the document. Your ballot is locked to a future round of the drand clock, run by Cloudflare, EPFL, Kudelski and others who have nothing to do with Zcash. Nobody can open it early, us included. Every registration, ballot and result sits in a public log, and your browser can recount it.

Zcash Agora is an independent project. It is not a Zcash organization, not an operator of the coinholder vote, and not paid by the dev fund. The code is public, from the protocol to this deployment. What it leaks is written down too, in the comparison. The short version is on how it works.

What it is, and what it is not

  • It is a second number next to the coin count, on the same question.
  • It binds no one. It allocates nothing and changes no rule.
  • It can be recounted by anyone, with no key and no permission.
  • Passports are the first proof of personhood it accepts, not the last.

The first poll is a test

The first poll tries the tool on the question with the clearest coinholder answer: do you want Zcash Shielded Assets? Its result decides nothing. What we want to learn is whether people in Zcash will scan a passport to be counted once, and whether the vote and the recount work with real phones. If they do, later polls can take up the questions of the day.
