Why this exists

A poll that weighs every voice by a balance answers one question: what does the money want? It cannot answer the other question the same decisions raise: what do the people want? Zcash Agora exists to ask the second one, with the same rigour as the first, and to put the two answers side by side.

What the coinholder poll measured

Since 2025, Zcash has taken governance signals from coinholder polls: a wallet proves it held shielded ZEC at a snapshot block and votes with that weight. The poll everyone quotes is the protocol feature sentiment poll of February 2026, and the figure everyone quotes from it is 98.6% against Zcash Shielded Assets. The same question was put, the same week, to the Zcash Foundation's advisory panel. Here is what sits behind the two figures, from the forum threads themselves.

The turnout is a forum member's calculation; each ballot is one vote transaction from one voting key, so the number of persons can only be lower. The ZCAP figures are the Foundation's results post.

The weight was concentrated. Twelve ballots make up half of it; the two largest, 90,999 and 90,644 ZEC, make up 14.7% on their own, and CipherScan traces both to one transparent address that received 202,077 ZEC from an exchange in December and shielded it on 24 December, a correlation they are careful not to call proof. The median ballot was 2,000 ZEC. The side that lost, 16,731 ZEC, was outweighed by the single largest ballot five times over. None of this is a flaw in the poll: it was built to weigh coins, and it did. It is a reason not to read its result as a count of people.

Two polls on one question, two opposite answers, and neither counts the persons of the ecosystem. One counts ZEC. The other counts the members of a panel of about 200 that the Zcash Foundation assembles by criteria of its own: past contributors, grant recipients, forum badge holders. For scale, the community forum had 14,742 registered accounts on 2 November 2026 and 842 active in the previous thirty days. Whatever the people of Zcash think about ZSAs, nobody has asked them.

How the number is used

The coinholder figure has become the governance figure of Zcash. It opens the case to end Zcash Community Grants: "In February 2026, coinholders voted 98.6% against Zcash Shielded Assets. ZCAP voted 70.4% for." It is the charge against ZCG's own plan, where a grant to ZSA work is "coinholder money" spent "after coinholders voted at 98.6% against", and where one reply asks "do you mean the 98% of the 7.25% turnout from all ZEC in circulation?" Since October 2026 coinholder votes also move money directly, through the coinholder-directed retroactive grants: a proposal passes on a simple majority of the ZEC that votes, with a floor of about 2% of the supply.

None of this is illegitimate. Coinholders carry the risk of the currency, and their view of its future deserves to be measured well. But when the coin count is the only count, two things follow. Whoever holds, or can buy, the most ZEC at the snapshot decides; the point is made from inside the debate, in the proposal to end the dev fund: "stake-based power can be bought". And the question of how many people stand behind a position never gets asked, so a figure cast by two hundred keys at most is read as the voice of the ecosystem.

Why Zcash, of all places, should count persons

Its roots are cypherpunk. Zcash exists so that a person can transact and be let alone, and that protection is the same for someone with a little and someone with a lot. A governance culture that weighs voices by holdings is one Zcash can borrow from shareholder meetings; it is not the one it came from. A count of persons is closer to it.

A passport cannot be bought in bulk. One person is one vote whatever they hold, and the vote costs nothing. The coinholder poll required moving ZEC into the Orchard pool during a registration window, so holders who keep their coins on an exchange, and people with no ZEC to move, were not counted there either.

The ecosystem already wants a people count. ZCAP is one: a few hundred members, chosen by one organization by criteria it sets, voting as members. Zcash Agora is the open version of the same idea: anyone with a passport, no list to be put on, run by nobody in the dispute, and recounted by anyone.

Its people should not have to vote in public. A forum poll is public and free to multiply. Here a ballot is sealed until the close and opens under a pseudonym; the registration proves a passport without disclosing it. A privacy coin's people deserve at least the privacy of a voting booth.

Why this method

One passport, one voice. A count of people needs a way to tell one person from two accounts, and forum handles, GitHub accounts and wallet addresses are free to create in any number. A passport is issued once per person by a state and can be verified on a phone with a zero-knowledge proof. ZKPassport produces that proof and a salted identifier unique to the document, blinded through a threshold network, so that neither this ledger nor the issuing state can recompute it. The costs are real: a biometric passport, an NFC phone, and a face match in the app. It is one proof of personhood; the registry format accepts others. The details.

A clock nobody here controls. The coinholder stack encrypts ballots to a key whose shares are held by a committee of Zcash organizations: the people with the most at stake in the outcome hold the key to the outcome. Here a ballot is time-locked to a future round of the drand beacon, run by Cloudflare, EPFL, Kudelski Security, Protocol Labs and a dozen others who have nothing to do with Zcash and do not know their beacon is used for this poll. The round's signature does not exist before the close, so nobody can open a ballot early, including us. After the close it is public, and that is what lets anyone recount.

A log anyone recounts. Every registration, every ballot, every refusal and the tally are in one append-only, signed log, mirrored in a public git repository and timestamped in Bitcoin. Your browser can redo every check: the passport proofs, the membership proofs, the opening of every ballot with the public drand round, and the count. If we vanished, the count would still come out. The protocol.

Not run by the parties it measures. Zcash Agora is a Lanterne Labs project: not a Zcash organization, not an operator of the coinholder vote, not funded by the dev fund. The code is public, from the voting protocol to this deployment and a draft ZIP describing it.

Honest about what it leaks. A relay sees your IP address, registering takes a face match, and at the close every ballot is readable under its pseudonym. Nothing is hidden about this; it is laid out next to the coinholder stack, question by question, in the comparison.

What this is, and what it is not

  • It is a companion. Every poll here is shown next to the coinholder poll on the same question when one exists, with the unit each one counts, its turnout, and what each one cannot know.
  • It is not binding. Nothing here allocates a zatoshi or changes a rule. It is a measurement offered to the people who will decide.
  • It is verifiable. A second number deserves at least the scrutiny the first one gets. Anyone can recount it.
  • It is open to other proofs of personhood. Passports are what works today on a phone; the registry takes other credentials as they come, and the owner's view is that a count gated by passports, even with the people it misses, beats a count gated by capital alone.

The first poll is a test

The first poll is a trial of the technology, not a decision on anything. We start with a sample question, the one that has the clearest coinholder answer and the least known human one: do you want Zcash Shielded Assets in the protocol? Its result is a suggestion, not a verdict. If the people's count looks like the coinholder count, that is worth knowing. If it does not, that is worth knowing more.

What we actually want to learn is whether people in the Zcash ecosystem would use a tool like this: whether they are willing to scan a passport to be counted once as a person, whether the vote and the recount work for them with real phones, and whether a people's count next to the coin count is something the ecosystem wants. If the answer is yes, later polls can take up the questions of the moment, such as the future of Zcash Community Grants, the dev fund, or any proposal the ecosystem wants a second count on.
