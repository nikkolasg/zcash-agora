Why this exists

A poll that weighs every voice by a balance answers one question: what does the money want? It cannot answer the other question the same decisions raise: what do the people want? Zcash Agora exists to ask the second one, with the same rigour as the first, and to put the two answers side by side.

Two questions, not one

Since 2025, Zcash has taken governance signals from coinholder polls: holders prove a shielded balance at a snapshot and vote with its weight. That is a legitimate instrument. Capital has skin in the game, and it should be heard.

But it is one instrument, and it measures one thing. In the February 2026 sentiment poll, the figure everyone quotes, 98.6% against Zcash Shielded Assets, is a share of the voting weight, cast by a turnout of roughly 7% of the circulating supply. The number of distinct humans behind it is unknown, and not by accident: the protocol has no notion of a person. Whether that weight belonged to a few hundred people or to a few dozen is not a footnote. It changes what the number means.

In the ZCG debate of October 2026 the same figure is used as "the coinholders have spoken". They have. The people have not been asked.

Two things Zcash cares about

Zcash's roots are cypherpunk: persons have a right to transact and to be let alone, and that right is the same for someone with a little and someone with a lot. Zcash is also a currency, and the people who hold it carry its risk; their view of its future is a signal worth measuring, and coinholder polling measures it well.

The two counts are both legitimate. They answer different questions, and the ecosystem decides best when it can see both. A decision taken on one of the two numbers is a decision taken blind in one eye.

What this is, and what it is not

  • It is a companion. Every poll here is shown next to the coinholder poll on the same question when one exists, with the unit each one counts, its turnout, and what each one cannot know.
  • It is not binding. Nothing here allocates a zatoshi or changes a rule. It is a measurement offered to the people who will decide.
  • It is not run by the parties it measures. Zcash Agora is a Lanterne Labs project, not a Zcash organization, not an operator of the coinholder vote, and not funded by the dev fund.
  • It is verifiable. Every registration, every ballot, every refusal and the result are in a public, signed, Bitcoin-timestamped log. Anyone can recount it. A second number deserves at least the scrutiny the first one gets.

Why passports

A count of people needs a way to tell one person from two accounts. Forum handles, GitHub accounts, e-mail addresses and wallet addresses are free to create in any number. A passport is a credential that a state issues once per person and that a phone can verify offline, with a zero-knowledge proof that discloses nothing beyond "this is a valid passport, and here is a number unique to it".

It is one mechanism, with real costs: it excludes people without a biometric passport or an NFC phone, and it takes a face match in the app, the price of a number that not even the issuing state can recognize. Humans matter more than coins, so we let the human speak. We are open to other proofs of personhood; the registry format accepts them. The details.

Why the ballots are sealed by an independent committee

The coinholder stack encrypts ballots to a key whose shares are held by a committee of Zcash organizations: the people with the most at stake in the outcome hold the key to the outcome. Here, a ballot is encrypted to a future round of the drand randomness beacon. That is a committee too, but an independent one: Cloudflare, EPFL, Kudelski Security, Protocol Labs and a dozen others, who have nothing to do with Zcash and do not know their beacon is used for this poll. The round's signature does not exist before the close, so nobody can open a ballot early, including us. After the close, it is public, and that is what lets anyone recount. The full comparison.

The first poll is a test

The first poll is a trial of the technology, not a decision on anything. We start with a sample question, the one that has the clearest coinholder answer and the least known human one: do you want Zcash Shielded Assets in the protocol? Its result is a suggestion, not a verdict. If the people's count looks like the coinholder count, that is worth knowing. If it does not, that is worth knowing more.

What we actually want to learn is whether people in the Zcash ecosystem would use a tool like this: whether they are willing to scan a passport to be counted once as a person, whether the vote and the recount work for them with real phones, and whether a people's count next to the coin count is something the ecosystem wants. If the answer is yes, later polls can take up the questions of the moment, such as the future of Zcash Community Grants, the dev fund, or any proposal the ecosystem wants a second count on.
