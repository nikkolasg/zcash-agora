Compared with the coinholder stack

The two systems answer different questions and should not be confused; but they are both voting systems for the same ecosystem, and they can be compared property by property. This page does that from the public documents only: the coinholder stack's draft ZIPs and code, isoloir's specification, and drand's and ZKPassport's documentation. Every claim about the coinholder stack links to the sentence it rests on. Where we derive something from several sentences, we say so. Nothing here is a disclosure; it is a reading of specifications that have been public since March 2026.

The table

PropertyCoinholder voting (Valar Group stack)Zcash Agora (isoloir)
What is counted ZEC in the Orchard pool at a snapshot height: one ballot per 0.125 ZEC, holdings below that carry no weight; Sapling and transparent funds carry none [V1 "Ballot Scaling"; V3 "Eligibility"]. Persons: one valid passport, one ballot of weight one; holdings are never asked [I §5, §7].
Who can read a ballot before the close Any t of the n validators, t = ⌈n/2⌉+1, who "reconstruct the key and decrypt individual share ciphertexts" [V1 "Privacy Implications", trust assumptions]. The validators are, per the production config, 14 named organizations (Valar Group, Zcash Foundation, Shielded Labs, Zodl, Keplr, Zcash Labs and others) [V5]; so 8 of them. Nobody. Ballots are time-locked to a drand round whose signature does not exist before the close; there is no decryption key in the system [I §7.2]. The only party that could produce the signature early is a threshold of the League of Entropy's nodes (Cloudflare, EPFL, Kudelski, Protocol Labs, the Ethereum Foundation, UCL and others), none of which is a Zcash organization, and what they would learn is what everyone learns at the round [D].
Is the choice (yes/no) hidden? No. "Vote decisions are public inputs to the Vote Reveal Proof and appear in every share reveal transaction on-chain" [V2 "Privacy Implications: Decision and share count visibility"]; submission servers "learn … the proposal identifier, and the vote decision" for every share they handle [V2 "Per-server information"]. What is encrypted is the weight, not the choice. Yes, until the close: the choice is inside the time-locked blob; the proof only shows a commitment to it [I §7.2, §9.1]. After the close the choice is public under a pseudonym.
Is the weight hidden? By obfuscation under a collusion assumption. A ballot's weight is split into 16 shares, "the ballot count is split roughly evenly" [V2 "Open Design Questions"], each encrypted to the committee key and each "sent to exactly ⌈s/2⌉ of the s available submission servers" [V2 "Server Selection"]. Every share's upload carries "the blinded share commitments for all N_s shares" [V2 "Per-server information"], the same list for the 16 shares of one ballot. Derivation: a share has ⌈s/2⌉ copies and the honest servers outside a decrypting coalition number at most s−t = ⌊s/2⌋−1, fewer than the copies, so a decrypting coalition holds a copy of every share of every ballot, groups them by the shared commitment list, and decrypts each voter's exact balance. The specification's own "a compromised server learns at most one share's encrypted amount per vote commitment" [V2 "Per-Server Share Isolation"] holds for one server; it does not hold for the coalition that can decrypt. There is no weight. The protocol has no balance to hide and no key to decrypt one with.
Link from a ballot to an identity The on-chain address is unlinkable to the vote: the holder delegates to a fresh hotkey with a zero-knowledge proof and "an observer … cannot link them without knowledge of nk" [V1 "Unlinkability to on-chain identity"]. But "a vote chain validator sees the source IP, submission timestamp, connection correlation, and P2P propagation pattern of every transaction it receives" [V3 "Privacy Implications"], and the ZIPs give no Tor recommendation (the client library offers routed transports [V6]). Under the collusion above, an IP maps to an exact balance and a choice. The passport is unlinkable to the leaf (the proof reveals only the document's unique identifier), and the leaf to the ballot (zero-knowledge membership proof with a per-poll pseudonym) [I §6, §9, §17]. The relay a voter posts to sees an address and a time; the operator does not, when relays are used. Tor or a VPN protects the address; the ballot's secrecy does not depend on it.
Permanence of the exposure Share ciphertexts are on the vote chain for good; "post-quantum security of the El Gamal encryption layer is out of scope" [V1 "Non-requirements"]. The key shares do not expire: a future compromise of t operators has the same effect as collusion today. After the round, every ballot is public under a pseudonym by design, so there is nothing left to decrypt later. The links that stay hidden (passport ↔ leaf, leaf ↔ ballot) are protected by zero-knowledge proofs, not by encryption waiting for a key.
Double voting, and who sets the rule A vote proves that its notes were unspent at the snapshot against a nullifier set [V1 "Nullifier Sets"; V3 "Snapshot Configuration"]. That set, and the snapshot roots, are computed by the party that runs the round and the nullifier service [V3 "Snapshot Configuration", "Nullifier Service"]. Wallets "MUST accept a round entry only if at least one signature validates … v1 implementations require a single valid signature per round entry" [V4 "Signature Verification"], and "the admin signature scope covers an entry's ea_pk only" [V4 "Distribution"]. Nothing in the written procedure recomputes the set. One registration per passport identifier, refused otherwise (uid_used), and one ballot per pseudonym; both rules are checked by the ingest, re-checked by the authority before it opens anything, and re-checked by any auditor, from the public log alone [I §6, §7.4, §16]. There is no set computed off-line by one party that the count depends on.
Trusted setup Halo 2 proofs: no trusted setup. An election-authority key ceremony among the validators produces the committee key [V3 "Genesis Validator Setup"]. UltraHonk proofs on a universal reference string (Aztec's): no per-circuit ceremony and nothing run by us. No key ceremony at all: the "key" is a public beacon. The passport side rests on ZKPassport's certificate registry, which anyone can check against the issuing states' published certificates [Z].
Can the result be recomputed by anyone? Partially. Partial decryptions are posted on chain with proofs and combined by Lagrange interpolation [V1 "High-level summary", phase 5]; an outsider can verify them but cannot produce the sum without the committee. Published results are a verifiable floor conditional on a correct nullifier set. Yes, with no key: the log, the beacon of the round, and the audit tool. Every registration proof, every ballot proof, every refusal and every opening are recomputed; two correct tallies are identical [I §10.4, §16].
Withholding a ballot "Like any chain, the voting chain's validators control what enters blocks"; withheld ballots are detectable by comparing cast and revealed counts on chain. Every message gets a signed receipt naming its checkpoint before it is logged; a missing body is a public complaint; the relays keep copies and the audit compares [I §11.2, §11.3, §16].
Infrastructure A separate proof-of-authority chain, validators, submission servers, PIR servers, all operated by the same organizations ("submission servers are embedded in validator nodes" [V2 "Why Servers Are Co-located with Validators"]). One ingest process with Postgres, a public git repository, public Nostr relays, and two networks that are read and never written to by voters: Bitcoin (timestamps) and drand (the clock). No chain, token or sequencer of ours [I §1].
Who is excluded Anyone without Orchard ZEC at the snapshot; holdings under 0.125 ZEC [V1 "Ballot Scaling"]. Anyone without a biometric passport or an NFC phone [Z; eligibility].
What the issuing authority learns Nothing (there is none). The state that issued a passport can compute its non-salted identifier and learn that it registered here; never how it voted [I §6].
Changing one's vote Not addressed in the fetched drafts. Not in a time-locked poll: one ballot per voter [I §8].
Binding? Advisory, per the polls' own terms. Advisory, and shown next to the coinholder count.

The three points that matter

1. Who holds the key to the outcome

In the coinholder stack, the key that opens every ballot is held, in shares, by the organizations with the most at stake in the outcome, and the same organizations run the servers that receive the shares. The protocol relies on those servers not pooling their logs: the specification says a server learns at most one share per vote, and the delivery rule two pages away sends each share to half the servers. A majority coalition that can decrypt also holds every share. That is a trust assumption, not a cryptographic guarantee, and exploiting it leaves no trace.

In Zcash Agora there is no key. The "key" is the signature of a drand round that will exist at 12:00 UTC on the closing day and not before, produced by a consortium that does not know this poll exists and has no stake in it. If every Zcash organization in the world colluded, they could not open a ballot early.

2. What is actually encrypted

In the coinholder stack the choice is public and the weight is encrypted. The exact balance of a shielded Zcash user, the one quantity the Zcash protocol is built to hide, is what the voting layer puts under a committee key, forever, on a chain, without post-quantum protection. In Zcash Agora there is no balance to protect: the ballot is a choice and nothing else, hidden until the close and anonymous after it.

3. What the count depends on

The coinholder count depends on a nullifier set computed by one party and signed, in v1, by one key, with no written recomputation step. The Agora count depends on nothing computed off-line: eligibility is a tree of logged, individually proven registrations, and the audit re-verifies every one of them, passport proof included. Less cryptography was needed, not more: no committee, no ceremony, no PIR, no proof-of-authority chain, no trusted configuration.

What we do not claim

  • We do not claim the coinholder stack miscounted anything, nor that any operator colluded. We have no evidence of either and no way to have any; that is the point about trust assumptions.
  • We do not claim Agora is private against every party: the issuing state learns participation, a relay sees an address, and every ballot is public at the close under its pseudonym. Those are stated on every page.
  • We do not claim a count of passports is the right measure of the ecosystem's will. We claim it is a second measure, and that one eye is better than none.
  • We do not claim this comparison is complete. The coinholder drafts are long and some sections were summarized rather than read verbatim; corrections with a citation are welcome in the repository and will be applied.
