Compared with the coinholder stack

The two systems answer different questions: what the coins want, and what the people want. Both are voting systems for the same ecosystem, so the same questions can be put to each. Every claim about the coinholder stack links to the sentence it rests on in the draft ZIPs and the published configuration; where we derive something from several sentences, we say so.

Who is involved

Coinholder voting

  • Your wallet proves it held Orchard ZEC at a snapshot block and votes with a fresh key.
  • One committee of 14 Zcash organizations holds three roles: it receives your shares and sees your IP and your choice; it holds the key that opens the amounts, any 8 of 14; and it runs the vote chain and the tally. One collusion of 8 breaks all three.
  • The poll runner is one of them: it picks the snapshot, supplies the tree roots (untrusted) and signs the round configuration wallets accept.
  • 3 PIR servers, run by the same organizations, tell wallets whether their notes are still unspent, and see who asks.
  • The vote chain is the committee's public record.

Zcash Agora

  • Your phone runs the ZKPassport app: the passport is read and proven there, with a face match. Your browser proves you are on the list and seals your ballot to the closing time.
  • 3 public Nostr relays, run by others, carry your messages. They see your IP, as any relay does.
  • The ledger, one server of ours, reads the relays, checks every proof, signs a receipt and appends to the log. It never talks to a voter.
  • The public log, a git repository, holds every registration, ballot, refusal and tally.
  • drand and Bitcoin are read, never written to: one is the clock that opens the ballots, the other timestamps the log.

Six questions, both answers

1. Who can read my ballot before the close?

Coinholder voting

Your choice is never sealed: "vote decisions are public inputs … and appear in every share reveal transaction on-chain". Every submission server that handles a share of yours sees the decision.

Your amount is encrypted to a key shared among the validators, any ⌈n/2⌉+1 of whom can reconstruct it. With 14 in production, that is 8 Zcash organizations.

Zcash Agora

Nobody, including us. The ballot is encrypted to a future round of the drand beacon; the key is the round's signature, which does not exist before the closing time. It is produced by the League of Entropy (Cloudflare, EPFL, Kudelski, Protocol Labs and others), none of them a Zcash party, none aware of this poll.

At the close the round is public for everyone at once, and every ballot opens under its pseudonym.

2. If that threshold is broken, what do they learn about my choice?

Coinholder voting

Nothing new: the choice was public from the start, on the chain and at the servers. No threshold protects it; the ZIP weighs encrypting it and declines on circuit cost.

Zcash Agora

They could open the ballots early, and learn what everyone learns at the close: a choice under a pseudonym that links to no passport and no address. Nothing they can alter, nothing to sell. The worst case of the threshold is a question of timing.

3. If that threshold is broken, what do they learn about my coin holdings?

Coinholder voting

Your exact balance. Your weight is split into 16 encrypted shares; each share is sent to 7 of the 14 servers, and the servers are the validators themselves. Any 8 of them therefore hold every share of every voter, see the same list of 16 commitments on each, group them, and decrypt. (Our derivation from these three rules; the ZIP's own answer is that splitting keeps the EA from linking shares, which holds for the EA alone, not for the EA that also runs the servers.)

The balance of a shielded user is the one number the Zcash protocol exists to hide.

Zcash Agora

Nothing. There is no amount anywhere in the system: your holdings are never asked, proven or encrypted. A ballot is a choice and nothing else.

4. What do they know about my IP address?

Coinholder voting

  • Normal operation: "a vote chain validator sees the source IP, submission timestamp, connection correlation, and P2P propagation pattern of every transaction it receives"; the PIR operator sees your IP and that you take part.
  • One corrupted node: it is also a submission server, so it pairs your IP with your choice and one encrypted share.
  • Threshold broken: IP, choice and exact balance, together, in the hands of the organizations that hold the key.

Zcash Agora

  • Normal operation: the relay you post to sees your IP and a sealed envelope, like any relay. Our ledger reads the relays and never sees a voter's address; the page calls no server of ours.
  • One corrupted relay: it keeps your IP next to the envelope it carried; at the close that envelope opens, so it can pair your IP with your choice. It still learns no passport: registration went to a different relay, and nothing links the two. Tor or a VPN removes even that.
  • Threshold broken: drand never sees you. Same as above, a few days earlier.

5. What stops someone from voting twice?

Coinholder voting

Voting twice with the same note on the vote chain: nullifiers. A note delegated twice, or a vote cast twice, is rejected by every node, and anyone replaying the chain can check that no nullifier appears twice.

Voting with a note already spent on Zcash: nothing in the protocol. Whether a note was still unspent at the snapshot is proven against a root of Zcash's spent-note tree, and that root is supplied by the vote manager in the poll-creation transaction; the chain rejects it only if the signer is not the vote manager. No validator, proof or wallet checks the root against Zcash mainnet: wallets accept the round on a single valid signature. A poll runner who supplies a root of their own choosing, with a PIR database to match, lets spent notes vote, and every such vote verifies. The same holds for the note-tree root, which decides which notes exist at all.

What exists outside the protocol: a script that rebuilds the spent-note tree from a Zcash node and compares its root with the chain's, and an admin dashboard that asks the vote manager to tick a box saying they ran it before signing the round. The tick is a statement, not a proof; the chain accepts the round either way. Anyone can run the script; nobody has to. Whoever creates the poll decides the electorate, on trust.

Zcash Agora

One passport, one registration: the proof discloses a number unique to the document, and the log refuses a second registration with the same number. One registration, one ballot: each ballot carries a per-poll pseudonym derived from the registered key, and the log refuses a second ballot under the same pseudonym.

Both rules are checked by the ledger when it appends, and again by anyone who recounts: every registration and every refusal is in the public log.

6. Can I recount the result myself?

Coinholder voting

You can check it, not produce it. Each validator posts its partial decryption on the chain with a proof, and svoted query vote verify-tally checks the proofs and recomputes the totals. It needs a node of your own: the production nodes do not expose the RPC it uses.

Without 8 validators' partial decryptions there is no tally; the round "verifies as having no tally". And the check says nothing about whether the snapshot roots were the real ones (question 5).

Zcash Agora

Yes, with no key and no permission: the public log, the drand round of the close, and the audit in your browser. It re-verifies every passport proof and every membership proof, opens every ballot with the public round, counts, and compares with the posted tally. If we vanished, the count would still come out.

Scope

  • This compares the two designs as published. It says nothing about any operator's conduct; a threshold assumption leaves no trace either way.
  • Agora's own exposures are on every page: a relay sees an address, registering takes a face match, and every ballot is readable at the close under its pseudonym.
  • Passports are one measure of personhood, coins are another. The two counts are meant to be read together.
  • Corrections with a citation are welcome in the repository and will be applied.
