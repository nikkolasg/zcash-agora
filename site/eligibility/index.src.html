Who can vote

Anyone with a valid biometric passport, of any country, of any age. One person, one vote, in every poll on this ledger.

The rule

  • A passport (not an identity card, not a residence permit, for now) with a chip, unexpired on the day you register.
  • No minimum age, no nationality rule. The poll asks what people think; it does not ask which people.
  • The registry remembers a number unique to your passport, so the same passport cannot register twice. It does not remember your name, your nationality, your birth date or your photo, because it never receives them.

Why a passport at all

A count of people needs a way to tell one person from two accounts. Without it, the count is of accounts, and accounts are free. Forum handles, GitHub accounts, e-mails, phone numbers, wallet addresses: all of them can be made by the dozen, and a poll that counts them measures who had the most afternoons to spare.

A passport is the one credential most adults on earth hold that a state issued once per person, that carries a cryptographic signature of that state, and that a phone can verify offline. With ZKPassport, the verification and the proof happen on your phone; what leaves it is a proof that the rule holds and a number derived from the document. Nothing else.

What it costs

  • Some people cannot take part. No biometric passport, no NFC phone, a passport whose issuing country does not publish its signing certificates: no vote. We know that is a real exclusion, and that it falls unevenly on the world.
  • The issuing state could learn that you registered. The unique number is derived from the document data, the site and the registry. A state that keeps the chip data of its passports can compute their numbers and compare them with the public list: it would learn that you registered, never how you voted. ZKPassport also offers a salted number, computed with a secret spread over independent servers, that no one can recompute from the passport alone; it needs a face match at registration, and we are evaluating it.
  • You trust ZKPassport's app and certificate registry. The SDK and the verifier are open source and the ledger re-verifies every proof; the registry of state certificates is public and checkable. We still name it as a dependency.

Why we accept those costs

Humans matter more than coins. A poll that weighs a voice by a balance lets the coin speak; this one lets the human speak, whatever they hold and even if they hold nothing. A count of passport holders, with its exclusions stated, is a count of people. That is what we are after, and passports are the first way we found to get it.

Passports are one proof of personhood, not the proof. The registry is a provider and a rule; a new provider is a new verifier, not a new protocol. If a proof of personhood exists that is more inclusive and as hard to forge (another document type, a web-of-trust with real guarantees, a proof-of-humanity you would stake your reputation on), we want to hear about it, and the ledger can host a second registry next to this one. What every registry here has in common: it counts people.

What you are not asked

  • Whether you hold ZEC, or how much.
  • Your name, nationality, age, or address.
  • An account anywhere.

One registration, every poll

You register once per ledger. Every poll on it uses the same registry, and your pseudonym in each poll is derived separately, so nothing links your ballot in one poll to your ballot in another. Keep the key your browser made (passkey or 12 words): it is what lets you vote in the next poll from another device.
